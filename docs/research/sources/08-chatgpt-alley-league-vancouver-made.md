## Executive Summary

**Best judge-ready frame:** not "a protest jersey," but **a repeatable design system that turns Vancouver civic evidence into wearable football artifacts**. That framing matches the event brief almost exactly: Vancouver Made is judging novelty of process and output, wants a project that captures a Vancouver story, and explicitly says the system behind the work matters as much as the final object. The fashion track also materially rewards manufacturability, with the winning design produced as ten full kits.

**Best hero concept: Pump & Dump FC.** It is the cleanest political strike for this room because it is legible, local, sharp, and safer than grief-centred concepts. It draws from the mechanics of pump-and-dump fraud as defined by Investor.gov and BCSC, and from Vancouver's long association with junior-resource speculation through the old Vancouver and later venture-exchange ecosystem.

**Winning formula:** one hero club, a credible civic-research pipeline, a manufacturable kit, a fake-but-plausible sponsor system, a merch layer, and a tight presentation spine. The city you are critiquing is already documenting the conditions that make this kind of work legible: Vancouver is on the unceded territories of the Musqueam, Squamish, and Tsleil-Waututh Nations; the city's World Cup by-law package explicitly foregrounded operations, safety, security, branding, brand protection, and a "clean and welcoming environment"; and the city's current World Cup cost backgrounder projects core and essential municipal costs in the range of **$320 million to $338 million** for seven matches and related events.

**Main watchouts:** do not use Indigenous forms, symbols, or motifs unless Indigenous artists are authoring that work; do not make serial-killer or victim merch; do not use FIFA marks or imply official affiliation; do not let parody drift into trademark confusion; and remember that football's actual rules prohibit political, religious, or personal slogans on playing equipment in controlled match contexts.

**Recommended submission shape:** one hero club with three kits, one scarf, one source-card wall, one process board, one fake store page, one short motion piece, one printable leave-behind, and a slide deck built around the sentence: **They asked for a Vancouver kit. We made the receipt.** The rest of Alley League can appear as expansion teams proving scale, not as unfinished distractions.

## Strategic Thesis and Judge-Facing Story

### One-Sentence Thesis

**Alley League is a Vancouver protest-football system that turns civic records, financial fraud language, and city-branding residue into wearable evidence.**

### Judge-Facing Sixty-Second Pitch

Vancouver Made asked what a Vancouver kit would look like. Most people will answer with mountains, ocean, and safe civic branding. We answered with the paperwork. Alley League is a protest-football system that turns Vancouver civic evidence into wearable artifacts. Our hero club, Pump & Dump FC, translates the mechanics of stock fraud and the city's finance mythology into a manufacturable kit: prospectus footnotes become striping, hype language becomes pattern, disclaimers become hem text, and every design element links to a QR source card. The output is a football club that looks official at first glance and collapses into evidence up close. The process is the real innovation: receipt to token to pattern to mockup to source card. It is a Vancouver story, but not the tourism version. It is the invoice.

### Thirty-Second Elevator

This is not a fan jersey. It is a counterfeit civic merch system. Pump-and-dump fraud language, Vancouver finance history, and host-city branding all get turned into a football identity with receipts attached. One hero club, three kits, a store, a source archive, and a clear pipeline the judges can score.

## Hero Concept and Merch Architecture

### Pump & Dump FC

The concept works because it binds a recognizable fraud structure to a Vancouver-specific economic mythology. Investor.gov defines pump-and-dump schemes as false or misleading hype used to inflate demand before insiders sell at the top and leave other investors holding losses. BCSC and InvestRight use similar language and note that current scams increasingly use social media and private messaging. TMX's own history notes the 1999 realignment in which the Vancouver and Alberta exchanges became the Canadian Venture Exchange, later TSX Venture Exchange.

**Club line:** BUY THE RUMOUR. SELL THE CITY.

**Design logic:** The club should read like polished sportswear from distance and like market manipulation from two feet away. The joke is not generic finance. The joke is **Howe Street as civic atmosphere**: prospectus language, private-placement swagger, speculative mining-energy residue, and the public as exit liquidity.

### Crest

**Structure:** circular badge with a deflating football that doubles as a candlestick chart.  
**Inner devices:** broken arrow, piggy-bank crack, prospectus footer line, and a tiny "halt" bar.  
**Ring text:** PUMP & DUMP FC — VANCOUVER  
**Sub-mark:** standalone ticker glyph **$VAN?**

**Construction rules:**
- No resemblance to FIFA marks, tournament trophy silhouettes, or official host-city identity.
- Keep the badge geometric and flat enough for embroidery.
- Use one-colour and two-colour variants only; do not rely on gradients.

## Ethics, Legal Boundaries, and Risk

### Core Guardrails

The strongest protest line here is **systems first, victims never as texture**. That is not just moral cleanliness; it is strategic clarity. Vancouver's civic and historical record already gives you more than enough material without borrowing sacred or traumatic imagery.

### Red-Team Slide Copy

**WHAT WE REFUSED TO DO**
- We did not use Indigenous forms, symbols, or textile motifs without Indigenous authorship.
- We did not make victim imagery, serial-killer merch, or trauma into surface decoration.
- We did not use FIFA marks, host-city marks, or copied sponsor identities.
- We did not sell a tourism version of Vancouver.

**WHAT WE USED INSTEAD**
- Public documents
- Fraud language
- Street grids
- Viaduct geometry
- Event-zone bureaucracy
- Municipal invoices
- Redactions
- Source cards

### Legal Notes

In Canada, copyright fair dealing explicitly includes parody and satire. It does **not** erase trademark risk. The Trademarks Act still turns on confusion, including whether the use could lead people to infer the goods or services come from the same source. FIFA's own IP guidelines state that FIFA holds all rights in relation to the World Cup and warn that the guidelines are general only, not permission. IFAB's Law 4 also reserves space to sanction political, religious, or personal slogans on playing equipment in actual football contexts.

### Risk Register

| Risk | Why it matters | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|---------------|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Indigenous appropriation | project becomes ethically incoherent fast | Medium | Severe | no Indigenous motifs unless Indigenous-led; use documents and civic forms instead |
| Victim-cosplay / grief mining | political backlash; harms credibility | Medium | Severe | no faces, no names, no red-hand mimicry, no serial-killer references |
| FIFA or sponsor confusion | IP and affiliation problems | Medium | High | avoid marks, trophy shapes, official phrases, near-clone logos |
| Defamation | saying the quiet part too specifically | Low | High | critique systems and archetypes; do not accuse named living persons without sourced basis |
| Overcrowded narrative | judges stop tracking the idea | High | High | one hero club, expansion as proof-of-system only |
| Non-manufacturable details | fashion-track weakness | Medium | High | keep crest simple, use sublimation, set realistic placement zones |
| Offline failure | room-tech collapse | Medium | High | local PDF, printed board, USB, phone backup |
| Message mismatch with brief | seen as only oppositional | Medium | Medium | anchor every critique in a real Vancouver record and a clean visual system |
| Actual-playing-kit rule conflict | if someone reads it as an official match uniform | Low | Medium | label as concept/protest/fashion artifact; note IFAB limits for official play |
| Labelling non-compliance if sold | avoid fake retail claims | Medium | Medium | keep claims accurate; use proper fibre/dealer/origin info if producing physical goods |

### Final Sixty-Second Script

> Vancouver Made asked for a Vancouver kit. Most versions of that idea go straight to mountains, ocean, and civic branding. We went the other way. Alley League is a protest-football system that turns Vancouver records into wearable evidence. Our hero club is Pump & Dump FC, built from the language of stock fraud, speculative finance, and host-city branding. Investor and securities sources define the fraud mechanics; local urban records ground the Vancouver story; and every design choice is traceable through a source-card pipeline. The home kit uses hype language as striping. The away kit is the crash. The third kit maps shell-company logic. We made a manufacturable system, not just a moodboard: crest rules, colour specs, flats, tech-pack logic, merch, packaging, and a fake store. The point is simple. If the city is going to sell itself through football, we can at least make the invoice visible.

### Final Thirty-Second Closer

> This project wins if the judges can say three things in one breath: new object, clear process, real Vancouver. Pump & Dump FC gives you all three. It looks like a club, reads like a scam, and cites like a report.
