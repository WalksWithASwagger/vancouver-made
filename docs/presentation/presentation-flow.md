## Eye on the prize

Two deliverables, nothing else:
1. **The jersey.** One kit that represents Vancouver. Any format.
2. **A short presentation.** Walk inspiration, design thinking, process, concept. Short
   is fine. It just has to show the story behind the jersey.

Judged on: strength of the idea, how well we explain it, specificity (generic loses),
Vancouver-uniqueness, and a design that reads without a caption.

**The submission is one jersey: Pump & Dump FC.** The other eight kits are not the
pitch. They are the proof the method repeats. Lead with the one. Show the system once.

## The IP line (a disqualifier, not a style note)

No real FIFA, club, league, brand, or sponsor logos, names, crests, slogans, or marks.
We **evoke** the grammar of official sports branding (lockups, sponsor bars, kit-maker
spec type) and build our own original marks. We never reproduce a real one. Before this
deck ships: pull the stray Nike swoosh off the Canva mockups (see
`docs/deliverables/mockups/README.md`), and gut-check every slide for a real logo.

## The flow (8 slides, ~5 minutes, hero-led)

Each slide notes the job it does. Between them the deck answers all four brief questions
(Q1 to Q4) and hits every rubric line.

**1. The hook.** "They asked for the Vancouver story. We finished the sentence."
Land that this is a Vancouver kit and a refusal of the souvenir. Name the club: Pump &
Dump FC. Land acknowledgement as substance, not throat-clearing: made on unceded
xʷməθkʷəy̓əm, Sḵwx̱wú7mesh, səlilwətaɬ territory.
*Job: set the frame. Open the loop.*

**2. The jersey.** Put the hero flat on screen and shut up for a second. A blackout
"city pride" kit that resolves into a prospectus the longer you look. Let the design
talk first.
*Job: Q2, what it communicates with no words. The deliverable, front and centre.*

**3. What part of Vancouver.** The spectacle itself: the gap between the broadcast
version and the bill. The World Cup as a pump and dump. Hype the city, bill the public,
take the exit, leave the residents holding the bag. Ground it: VSE "Scam Capital of the
World," Howe Street, ~12× price-to-income (Demographia 2025).
*Job: Q1, specific not generic.*

**4. Could only come from here.** Run the test out loud. The VSE history is real. The
housing numbers are Vancouver's. Culture jamming was born here (Adbusters, 1989). The
tournament is live in 2026, the hype is moving the market right now. A generic chart
kit could come from anywhere. This one could only come from Vancouver.
*Job: Q3, the uniqueness test, answered yes.*

**5. The method.** Mimic the official polish. Invert the payload. Bake the receipt on
the hem. Show the parts: the crest (ball / candlestick / condo stack), the sponsor bar
(`PUMP & DUMP CAPITAL` / `WE WIN IF YOU LIVE HERE OR NOT`), roles not names
(DEVELOPER, FLIPPER, LANDLORD, RENOVICTOR), the hem citation. Then the reveal: this is
one of nine kits built on the same repeatable move. A receipts engine, not a t-shirt.
*Job: process and novelty (the rubric's centre of gravity). Collection as proof.*

**6. Why you'd be proud to wear it.** "You're not in the stands. You're the bag." Pride
here means naming the mechanism, refusing the celebration jersey, standing with the
people getting priced out instead of the spectacle pricing them out.
*Job: Q4.*

**7. The lineage.** "We didn't invent this. We joined a line." 1968 Smith and Carlos,
the podium as protest surface. 1989 Adbusters, culture jamming born in Vancouver. 2012
Brandalism, perfect-mimicry fake ads over sponsors. 2010 the Cowichan sweater, whose
culture gets sold. We borrow no sacred imagery. We use the coloniser's own paperwork.
*Job: credibility and care. Pre-empt the "is this just a stunt" question.*

**8. The receipts and the close.** The public-record numbers, cited, every one
verified before this is shown (the `[confirm]` flags in `src/data/receipts.js` get
cleared first). Close on the line: "The part that doesn't fit on a souvenir." Optional:
a QR to the live site.
*Job: the craft is the citation. End on the provocation, not a thank-you slide.*
